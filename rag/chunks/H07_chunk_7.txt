Morbidity outcomes were eligible if they were linked to healthcare utilisation, such as hospital admissions, emergency department visits, ambulance call-outs, or outpatient visits. Healthcare cost outcomes were included when directly related to healthcare utilization during heat exposure. Disease classifications followed the ICD-10 system (codes A00–Z99) and encompassed heat-sensitive categories such as cardiovascular, respiratory, renal, genitourinary, metabolic, and heat-related illnesses Studies were included if they reported outcomes on a daily resolution and provided effect estimates such as relative risk, odds ratio, percent change, or incidence rate ratios with a 95% confidence interval (CI) based on empirical epidemiological data from observed historical health and temperature records. We excluded studies reporting only self-reported symptoms, functional performance, or subjective wellbeing, as well as those focusing solely on physiological parameters or biomarkers without associated health outcomes. Studies using simulated, projected, or scenario-based data were excluded. Studies reporting indirect, productivity, or mortality costs (e.g., value-of-statistical-life estimations or economic valuations of years of life lost) were also excluded. 5) Study designs: Eligible studies used observational ecological study designs such as time series, case crossover, or case time-series designs to compare risks over different exposures or time periods. Only peerreviewed, original studies in English were included. We excluded studies based on self-reported or questionnaire-based exposure or outcome assessment, as well as experimental, simulation-based, or laboratory studies. Also excluded were reviews, editorials, commentaries, theses, conference abstracts, and non-English publications. 2.2. Search strategy A comprehensive literature search was conducted with guidance from the university librarian across six databases: PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Scopus, CINAHL, and AgeLine. The search strategy was structured around the PECO framework and applied keywords and Boolean operators covering the following domains (full search terms listed in Appendix 1, Table S4): Population (e.g., “Older people”, “Aged”), Exposure/Comparator (e. g., “Heat”, “Hot weather”, “High Temperature”), Outcomes (e.g., “Health outcomes”, “Morbidity”, “Mortality”, “Health care cost”). Within each domain, search terms were combined using “OR” to include all relevant variants. These groups were then connected using the “AND” operator. Pilot testing of the search strategy was conducted during its development. Based on preliminary findings, outcome-related terms were refined to include specific morbidity indicators such as hospitalisation, ambulance call-out, emergency department visits, and outpatient visits. To assess the completeness and validity of the search, we reviewed the reference lists of 25 relevant systematic reviews identified through targeted searches (Appendix 1, pp. S3–S12). This step helped identify additional eligible primary studies not retrieved through the initial search and confirmed adequate conceptual coverage. The final search was conducted for studies published between January 1, 1990, and May 31, 2023. An updated search was performed on March 15, 2025, to capture newly published literature. 2.3. Risk of bias assessment The RoB for individual studies was assessed using a modified version of the Office of Health Assessment and Translation (OHAT) RoB Tool, following the Navigation Guide for systematic reviews in environmental health (OHAT, 2019, Liu et al., 2022, Johnson et al., 2014). Each study was evaluated across eight domains: exposure assessment, outcome assessment, confounding, selection bias, incomplete or missing data, selective reporting, conflict of interest, and other sources of bias. For each domain, risk was rated as “low,” “probably low,” “probably high,” or “high” risk based on pre-specified criteria (Appendix 1, Table S5). The intermediate categories (“probably low” and “probably high”) were applied when information was insufficient for a definitive judgment but provided indirect evidence toward lower or higher risk, respectively, consistent with Navigation Guide conventions. The overall RoB was determined based on three key domains: exposure, outcome, and confounding (Liu et al., 2022). The rationale for overall bias ratings is provided in Appendix 1, Table S6. Six reviewers (SG, YL, MB, JLi, YH, YS) independently conducted RoB assessments and resolved disagreements through discussion. 2.5. Synthesis of the evidence Study findings were synthesised both quantitatively and narratively. A DerSimonian and Laird random-effects meta-analysis was conducted (DerSimonian and Laird, 1986) to pool effect estimates from studies that provided relative risk (RR), odds ratio (OR), incidence rate ratio (IRR), or other effect estimates that could be converted to RR (e.g. percentage change or excess risk) (DerSimonian and Laird, 1986).
