The association between floods and the risk of typhoid in one year in a city in Indonesia was not statistically significant (RR 1.65 [0.88, 3.08]); however, there was an increased risk of paratyphoid (RR 4.52 [1.90, 10.73]) (Vollaard et al., 2004). Hand-foot-mouth disease was investigated in one study in mainland China (Hu et al., 2018b), which found an increased risk during 0–1 month post exposure to floods (RR 1.18 [1.03, 1.35]). While the risk of HAV increased (RR 1.40 [1.11, 1.15]) on the 15th day post a flood event in Anhui, mainland China, no significant association was found between this flood event and HEV (RR 0.94 [0.67, 1.31]) (Gao et al., 2016). The risk of malaria incidence was increased after flood events in Sudan, mainland China, and Uganda, although the time frames varied. Cardiovascular diseases One study in the USA indicated that the hospitalization risk of CVD increased (RR 1.01 [1.00, 1.03]) during 0–14 days post floods (Deng et al., 2022). However, no significant association was found between flood and the risk of acute CVD (1.25 [0.81, 1.92]) (Vanasse et al., 2016). 3.3.5. Skin and eye diseases Three studies explored the associations between flood and skin diseases (Fig. 4). (Chen et al., 2021a; Huang et al., 2016; Saulnier et al., 2018) One study estimated the risk of overall skin diseases (Huang et al., 2016), while the other studies estimated the risks of skin infection and atopic dermatitis. All studies found an increased risk of skin diseases post flood events. The time frames varied from a week to three months post floods. The associations for eye diseases were estimated in two studies. One study in Taiwan found an increased risk of overall eye diseases during 0–10 days post flood exposure (Huang et al., 2016). Another study in mainland China found a twofold increased risk of acute hemorrhagic conjunctivitis in 0–10 days post floods (Liu et al., 2020). 3.3.6. Adverse birth and pregnancy outcomes The associations of floods with adverse birth outcomes were estimated in two studies (Fig. 4). One study in Canada estimated the associations in the year of a flood event (Hetherington et al., 2021), and the study in the USA estimated the association during 0–4 years post a flood event (Tong et al., 2011). The associations for preterm birth and small for gestational age were estimated by both studies. In the two studies, the risk of preterm birth was found to increase (RR 1.09 [1.03, 1.16]) during 0–4 years post the flood event in the USA, while the other associations were statistically insignificant. Additionally, the risk of low birth weight was also found to increase (RR 1.11 [1.03, 1.21]) during 0–4 years post the flood event in the USA. 3.3.7. Malnutrition The risk of malnutrition among children aged 6–59 months was estimated by Rodriguez-Llanes et al. in two studies in a district in Orissa, India (Fig. 4). (Rodriguez-Llanes et al., 2011, 2016) These studies were based on the same population and flood events, but with different time frames and sample sizes. They found that the risks of stunting and being underweight were higher in exposed group in the second month post exposure; however the association for wasting was statistically insignificant (Rodriguez-Llanes et al., 2011). At one year after the flood event, they found that there was an increased risk of being underweight and wasting (Rodriguez-Llanes et al., 2016). 3.3.8.
